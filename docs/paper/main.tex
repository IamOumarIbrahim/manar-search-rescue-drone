\markboth{MANAR Technical Preprint}{Ibrahim: MANAR---An Evidence-Scoped Software Testbed for Human-Supervised Aerial Search and Rescue}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\normalfont\small
Search-and-rescue unmanned aircraft must automate repetitive search tasks without allowing uncertain perception to make high-consequence decisions. This paper presents MANAR (Multi-sensor Autonomous Navigation and Aerial Rescue), an evidence-scoped software testbed that separates implemented source, measured software behavior, and planned flight capabilities. The current artifact contains a C++ control core with Haversine navigation, nearest-neighbor sector ordering, operator-preserved manual ordering, plan locking, and Return-to-Launch semantics, together with ONNX Runtime detector harnesses. A fixed-seed study of 500 synthetic sector layouts finds that greedy tours are $4.6\%$--$11.1\%$ longer on average than tours refined by 2-opt; 2-opt is treated as a local-search reference, not a global optimum. A diagnostic zero-shot comparison on 26 ten-second clips (6,240 frames) reports mean model-session latency of $29.25\pm0.85$ ms for YOLO11n and $50.53\pm3.15$ ms for D-FINE-N across clip means. At least one person event occurs in 12 of 15 positive-designated clips for YOLO11n and 6 of 15 for D-FINE-N; neither produces an event in 11 negative-designated clips. Because the corpus lacks bounding-box ground truth, these counts are not precision, recall, or average precision. No physical flight, calibrated multisensor fusion, or operational rescue performance is claimed.
\end{abstract}
