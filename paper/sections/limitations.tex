\section{Limitations and Future Work}

While MANAR establishes a deterministic C++ baseline and comprehensive architectural framework, several operational limitations warrant ongoing development:

\subsection{Environmental and Weather Boundaries}
MANAR does not claim all-weather operational capability:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Precipitation and Icing:} Heavy rain attenuates optical/acoustic signals, while dense wet snow and hail introduce propeller icing, airframe structural damage, and severe propulsion risk.
    \item \textbf{Atmospheric Obscurants:} Dense fog degrades visual/IR imaging; dust and sandstorms introduce optical backscatter and motor bearing abrasion.
    \item \textbf{Thermal and RF Clutter:} Solar-heated desert rocks, hot vehicle engines, and active fires create thermal false alarms. Canyons, metallic structures, and urban rubble induce multipath and bearing errors in passive RF localization.
    \item \textbf{Radar and Acoustic Contamination:} Wind-blown vegetation can mimic human micro-motion on FMCW Doppler maps, while airframe vibration and rotor noise can mask faint acoustic distress calls.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Active Search Endurance Constraints}
The $1.0\text{ hr}$ active search endurance target ($SYS\text{-}G\text{-}02$) depends strongly on aggregate payload mass, battery specific energy, ambient operating temperature, search velocity, hover verification frequency, onboard compute draw, and active guidance usage. Full empirical validation on the integrated airframe remains planned future work.

\subsection{Algorithmic Bounds and Future Work}
The greedy route optimizer executes in $O(n^2)$ time but does not guarantee global optimality; future iterations may explore local 2-opt search or exact Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP). Future work focuses on implementing the React/TypeScript GUI, developing PyTorch perception models, validating the Mamba SSM on field datasets, integrating PX4 Software-in-the-Loop (SITL) simulation, and conducting physical flight trials.
